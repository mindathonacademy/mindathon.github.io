The six areas

Challenges that train the mind

Each card below describes a cognitive area and how Mindathon provides opportunities to practise it. No shortcuts, no promises — just structured, regular practice through play.

Memory & Recall

Memory is the ability to hold information and bring it back when needed. Mindathon challenges give players opportunities to observe, remember and recall — sometimes under time pressure, sometimes after a distraction.

How Mindathon supports it: challenges that ask players to remember what they saw, heard or did — and use it correctly a moment later.

Focus & Attention

Focus means directing attention to one thing and keeping it there. Mindathon rounds are structured so that every challenge begins and ends — players must be present from the first moment to the last.

How Mindathon supports it: short, well-defined challenges that reward attention, with gameplay that discourages drifting.

Problem Solving

Problem solving is working through a question or situation to reach an answer. Mindathon challenges present problems that cannot be solved by reflex alone — they ask players to reason, compare and decide.

How Mindathon supports it: challenges with rules and constraints that require thought before responding.

Mental Speed & Accuracy

Mental speed is how quickly the mind processes information; accuracy is getting it right. Mindathon gives players the chance to practise both — responding quickly while keeping answers correct.

How Mindathon supports it: timed and paced challenges that balance speed against getting it right.

Mental Flexibility

Mental flexibility is the ability to switch between ideas, rules or approaches. Mindathon keeps things varied — the rules of one challenge may not apply to the next, so players learn to adapt.

How Mindathon supports it: rotating challenge types that require players to change strategy from round to round.

Confidence & Positive Attitude

Trying something new takes confidence. Mindathon creates a supportive environment where players are encouraged to respond, learn from mistakes and try again — building a positive attitude toward challenges.

How Mindathon supports it: a play format where every player participates, and every attempt is part of learning.
